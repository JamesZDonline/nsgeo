nsgeo 0.3.0 lets you fix a line's placement after import, keeps every pick on
its line when lines or grids move, and adds migration and new ways to fill
slices between lines. It also fixes a bandpass artefact at the bottom of each
trace. It is still **experimental**: keep backups of your site folders.

**Start with the
[getting-started guide](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/blob/v0.3.0/docs/guide/getting-started.md).**

## Install or upgrade

Download `nsgeo_qgis-0.3.0.zip` from **Assets** below, not the *Source code*
downloads, which will not install. Do not unzip it. In QGIS, choose **Plugins →
Manage and Install Plugins… → Install from ZIP**, choose the zip, and click
**Install Plugin**. Click **Yes** at QGIS's security warning. Then restart QGIS.

If you have 0.2.0 or 0.1.0, install the new zip the same way; QGIS replaces the
old version. Restart QGIS afterwards, and check that **Plugins → nsgeo → About
nsgeo** reports `plugin 0.3.0 · core 0.3.0`. 0.3.0 opens surveys made with
earlier versions. The first time it opens a site that has picks, it puts any
pick that had drifted off its line back onto it, with its depth at the line's
current velocity, and the message bar says how many picks it updated. Surveys
saved by 0.3.0 can hold steps and settings that earlier versions don't know,
so keep to 0.3.0 once you have upgraded.

## Runs on

- QGIS 3.40 to 3.44, and QGIS 4.x. Its zip is checked by installing it into
  QGIS 3.40, 3.44 and 4.2.
- Windows and Linux. macOS is untested.

## What's new

### Lines and picks

- **Edit lines.** Right-click a grid in **nsgeo Survey** and choose **Edit
  lines…** for a table of every line on the grid. Change a line's label,
  offset, direction, start or grid in its row. Select several rows to set their
  start, **Snap to grid edge**, shift their offsets, flip their direction or
  move them to another grid together. **↑** and **↓** set the order the Survey
  panel shows. Nothing changes until **OK**, and then everything changes at
  once. A **Note** column points out a line that starts off its edge of the
  grid or lies outside its width.
  ([#29](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/29))
- **Move to grid.** Right-click a line, or a selection of lines, and choose
  **Move to grid** to put them on another grid. A line walked the other way
  lands at the new grid's far edge.
  ([#64](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/64))
- **Picks follow their line.** A pick is a trace and a time on its line. When
  you edit a grid or a line, or move a line to another grid, its picks move
  with it. A pick's depth and velocity follow the line's current velocity, so a
  new velocity or a fit you apply updates them. A pick dragged on the map goes
  back onto its line when you save the layer's edits; its note and other
  attributes stay editable.
  ([#35](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/35))
- **A grid's length takes its reversed lines with it.** Make a grid shorter or
  longer in **Edit grid…**, and the lines that start at its far edge move with
  that edge. The message bar says how many moved.
- **Slices keep up.** A slice that uses a line you edit or move is prepared
  again from the line's new place.
- **Grids start collapsed** in the Survey panel, and open when you open one of
  their lines. ([#133](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/133))

### Processing

- **Migration.** A new **Migration** group in **Add step** holds **Migration
  (Stolt f-k)**. It collapses diffraction hyperbolas back towards the objects
  that made them, which sharpens profiles and slices. Its **Velocity** starts
  at the line's own velocity and can be typed over; its **Aperture** (default
  45°) sets the steepest angle it moves energy along. If the velocity no longer
  matches the line's, the step's row says so. It refuses, and says why, when a
  line can't be migrated, such as a file recorded on a timer rather than a
  survey wheel. Put it in a preset to migrate every line in a slice cube.
  ([#123](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/123))
- **A cleaner bandpass.** The bandpass no longer folds the direct wave into the
  bottom of each trace, so late times are much cleaner after time-zero.
  ([#115](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/115))

### Slices

- **Interpolation replaces Fill radius.** The Slices panel's **Interpolation**
  box fills the cells between lines by **Kriging** (the default), **Inverse
  distance**, **Minimum curvature**, or **None** (only the cells a line
  crossed). **Max distance to data** is set in line spacings, 0.75 by default,
  with the metres shown beside it. **Smoothing X** and **Smoothing Y** smooth
  along each of the grid's axes. Each grid, and the whole site, remembers its
  own settings. ([#104](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/104))
- **Minimum curvature** draws the smoothest surface through the lines. It is
  slower, so while you move the depth the panel shows kriging, and it refines
  to minimum curvature once the depth rests.
  ([#122](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/122))
- **Exports** show their progress and can be cancelled. A cancelled export
  leaves no partial file.

## Known limits

- **macOS is untested.**
- **[#22](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/22):** unsaved edits in
  the picks layer can be lost when the grids' CRS changes. Save or discard
  picks-layer edits before you use any grid command: Add grid…, Edit grid…,
  Set velocity… or Remove grid.
- **[#23](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/23):** moving or renaming
  the site's GeoPackage on its own can lose data. Move, copy or back up the whole
  site folder, with QGIS closed.
- **[#40](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/40):** a dewow window of
  one sample, or a sliding background window of one trace, blanks the profile
  without a warning. Keep these windows at least two samples (or two traces)
  wide.

The guide's *Known issues* section explains each one and its workaround in full.
Also open: Whole site can refuse a very fine cell
([#107](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/107)), dragging the depth
slider in Whole site can lag on a large site
([#73](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/73)), and reopening a saved
QGIS project can bring back a second, stale copy of the site's layers
([#78](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/78)).
