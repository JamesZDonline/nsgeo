nsgeo 0.4.0 adds **Cut**, a tool that draws a line anywhere on a grid and shows
the vertical section beneath it. It also lets you measure and correct where
each line sits, makes the Time zero step set the clock, and makes slices at
fine cells faster and sturdier. It is still **experimental**: keep backups of
your site folders.

**Start with the
[getting-started guide](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/blob/v0.4.0/docs/guide/getting-started.md).**

## Install or upgrade

Download `nsgeo_qgis-0.4.0.zip` from **Assets** below, not the *Source code*
downloads, which will not install. Do not unzip it. In QGIS, choose **Plugins →
Manage and Install Plugins… → Install from ZIP**, choose the zip, and click
**Install Plugin**. Click **Yes** at QGIS's security warning. Then restart QGIS.

If you have 0.3.0, 0.2.0 or 0.1.0, install the new zip the same way; QGIS
replaces the old version. Restart QGIS afterwards, and check that **Plugins →
nsgeo → About nsgeo** reports `plugin 0.4.0 · core 0.4.0`. 0.4.0 opens surveys
made with earlier versions. Two things stop an older version reading a site
that 0.4.0 has saved:

- **After you open a site in 0.4.0, do not reopen it in 0.3.0.** The first
  time 0.4.0 opens a site, the picks table gains a `zero_ns` column. Nothing is
  converted: a missing zero means the radar's clock. 0.3.0 drops that column, so
  any pick made or moved on a profile that Time zero relabelled would then read
  0.5 to 1.5 ns off.
- **A site with cuts saves as schema version 2, and 0.3.0 refuses to open it.**
  It says the site "declares schema version 2, but this build reads version
  1". A site without cuts still saves as version 1.

A cube saved before 0.4.0 comes back shifted by the median line zero, so its
slices sit at the depths they showed before. The Slices panel says so.

## Runs on

- QGIS 3.40 to 3.44, and QGIS 4.x. Its zip is checked by installing it into
  QGIS 3.40, 3.44 and 4.2.
- Windows and Linux. macOS is untested.

## What's new

### Cuts

- **Cut tool.** Turn on **Cut** in the nsgeo toolbar and click points on a grid
  to draw a line. **Backspace** removes the last point, **Esc** cancels, and
  **Enter** or a right-click finishes. A cut must start inside a grid. The
  profile then shows the vertical section beneath the line, with the same
  hover, cursor and picks as a survey line.
  ([#172](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/pull/172))
- **Cuts live with their grid.** They appear in a `cuts` layer and under each
  grid in the **nsgeo Survey** panel, where you can rename and delete them.
  When you move, resize or rotate a grid, its cuts and their picks move with
  it. Removing a grid removes its cuts, and says so.
- **The section is read from the grid's slices,** at the Slices panel's cell,
  interpolation and smoothing. Under **Minimum curvature**, a kriging preview
  shows at once and the title says so. The solved section replaces it in the
  background, and the title counts the depths solved. While the grid prepares
  again, the profile says so.
- **What the map follows depends on the current layer.** With `cuts` current,
  hover, the cursor and selection follow cuts. Otherwise they follow survey
  lines, as before. Turning on **Cut** makes `cuts` current.
- **Picks on a cut** record the cut and the distance along it, and whether the
  section was solved or a kriging stand-in. A pick whose cut you delete stays
  where it was drawn, and selecting it says so.
  ([#170](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/170))
- **A cut's axis uses the clock of the cube it was read from.** A pick made on
  another clock is left out of the profile, and the title counts it.

### Line placement

- **Fit to grid edges.** **Edit lines…** has two new columns, **Scale** and
  **Destagger (m)**, and two new rows in its bulk panel. **Fit to grid edges**
  stretches or compresses each selected line so its first trace sits on the edge
  it starts at and its last trace on the other edge. The **Note** column flags
  a scale more than 20% from 1. At import, an opt-in box, **Stretch or compress
  each line to the grid's length**, does the same, and greys out **Start
  along**. A grid whose length changes refits its fitted lines.
  ([#3](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/3))
- **Destagger (m)** moves every trace of a line along its direction of travel,
  to remove the stagger between lines walked out and back. Fit lines before you
  destagger. Neither correction changes what the file recorded, and you can
  undo both in **Edit lines…**. **nsgeo Survey** shows a corrected line with
  its scale and shift, such as ` · ×0.954` or ` · destagger +0.15 m`.
  The map, the profile, picks, velocity fitting and slices all follow the
  corrected placement.
  ([#47](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/47))
- **Measure.** A **Measure** button in the **Destagger (m)** row works out the
  shift for the grid from its own lines, and gives a line its own shift where it
  is clearly off. It compares every line on the grid with its neighbours walked
  the other way, over every depth; the selection only chooses which rows get
  filled. The numbers are a starting point: nothing changes until **OK**. Lines
  it cannot use are named on the status line with the reason. While it runs the
  button reads **Stop**, and the rest of the dialog stays usable. A grid walked
  one way says at once that there is no stagger to measure.
  ([#159](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/159))

### Time zero

- **The Time zero step sets the clock.** The row it lands on reads 0 ns, so
  depths, velocity fits and slices measure from each line's first break. The
  profile's top row reads 0 ns. Without the step, the axis keeps the radar's
  clock, as before.
  ([#97](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/97))
- **Picks stay on their reflections** when you add, remove or re-threshold
  Time zero. Each pick records the zero it was measured from.
- **Velocity fits** are saved on the radar's clock with the zero of their
  results, so they follow the line's time zero. The fit strip's time-zero
  warning goes away once the step is in the stack. A fit that can't be refitted
  is listed and can be deleted, but is never applied.
- **Slices and exports record their clock.** A cube built with Time zero starts
  at 0 ns. **Whole site** warns in the site note, and names the grids without
  Time zero, when a site's grids are on different clocks. GeoTIFFs record their
  clock in `NSGEO_TIME_BASE` (`NSGEO_SITE_TIME_BASE` for a site export).

### Slices

- **Whole site never refuses a fine cell.** Past a size, it draws a coarser
  preview and says why in the panel, with the finest cell that would draw in
  full. A preview site refuses to export before any file dialog opens. Both
  export messages now name the cell and the pixel size.
  ([#107](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/107),
  [#112](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/112))
- **Pictures blend the cells around each pixel,** at the cell size, so a slice
  is drawn smooth when you zoom in. Coverage still shows whole counts, so
  one-cell-wide survey lines stay visible.
- **A faster minimum-curvature solver.** It is about 4 to 5 times faster at a
  0.05 m cell with the same slices. On real lines at 0.05 m it falls from 540
  iterations to 15. At a 0.2 m cell it is 1.4 to 2 times slower, so Whole site
  can show its kriging preview for a moment longer.
  ([#111](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/111))
- **A smaller cube.** A cube held in memory stores only the cells its lines
  cross, about 3 to 8 times less memory at fine cells, with the same slices.
  Saved cubes use the compact format, and cubes saved earlier still load.
  ([#113](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/113))
- **An honest memory warning.** For minimum curvature, the Slices panel's
  over-budget warning now counts the solver's plan beside the kriging preview,
  about twice the weights of inverse distance or kriging, so it comes on in time.
  ([#148](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/148))
- **This grid and Whole site agree.** They read a grid's saved line choice and
  preset the same way. A line you re-import rejoins the choice in both. Ticking
  every line in the chooser means every line, including lines imported later.
  Opening a grid from the site list uses the preset the site drew it with, and a
  refused preparation clears the map.
  ([#100](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/100),
  [#101](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/101),
  [#106](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/106),
  [#103](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/103))
- **The background solver lets go of a cancelled solve's memory at once,** not
  at the next solve, and its errors name their type, such as `minimum curvature
  failed: ValueError: …`.
  ([#120](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/120))

### Processing and the profile

- **A cleaner envelope.** The Hilbert envelope no longer wraps the top of a
  trace into the bottom.
  ([#116](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/116))
- **Bandpass removes DC.** A **Low** edge below the taper width used to pass
  part of DC (9.5% at 100 to 600 MHz, 71% at 50 to 600 MHz). The taper now
  starts at 0 MHz, so DC is always removed. **Low** = 0 is still a lowpass.
  ([#117](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/117))
- **Shift+scroll** over the map moves the depth one Step per wheel notch, also
  on a mouse whose wheel reports a notch in several small events.
  ([#109](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/109))

### Grids

- **A grid's velocity is optional.** The **Edit grid…** velocity box reads
  **from line headers** at 0, and OK no longer needs a velocity. A grid with no
  velocity uses each line's header dielectric. **Set velocity…** can clear a
  grid's velocity. A new grid no longer copies the first line's header
  velocity, and an untouched box keeps a fitted velocity at full precision.
  Import's **Note** column flags a header dielectric outside 1 to 81, such as
  `header ε 0 is outside 1–81: set a grid or line velocity`; the line still
  imports.
  ([#4](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/4))

### For code that uses the core library

- `compose`, `lattice_pixel`, `snap_azimuth` and `AXIS_SNAP_CELLS` are gone
  from the core's public API ([#107](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/107)).
- `SliceCube` holds packed blocks; read it with `slice_levels`, `level`,
  `values_at` and `coverage`, not by indexing `mean`
  ([#113](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/113)).
- `estimate_memory` now requires `n_covered` and `method`
  ([#113](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/113),
  [#148](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/148)).

## Known limits

- **macOS is untested.**
- **[#169](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/169):** a cut under
  minimum curvature takes about 100 s to finish on a fine grid, such as a 0.05 m
  cell with about 460 depths. A kriging preview shows at once, and the solved
  section replaces it.
- **[#22](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/22):** unsaved edits in
  the picks layer can be lost when the grids' CRS changes. Save or discard
  picks-layer edits before you use any grid command: Add grid…, Edit grid…,
  Set velocity… or Remove grid.
- **[#23](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/23):** moving or renaming
  the site's GeoPackage on its own can lose data. Move, copy or back up the whole
  site folder, with QGIS closed.
- **[#40](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/40):** a dewow window of
  one sample, or a sliding background window of one trace, blanks the profile
  without a warning. Keep these windows at least two samples (or two traces)
  wide.

The guide's *Known issues* section explains #22, #23 and #40 and their
workarounds in full.
Also open: dragging the depth slider in Whole site can lag on a large site
([#73](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/73)), and reopening a saved
QGIS project can bring back a second, stale copy of the site's layers
([#78](https://github.com/JamesZDonline/nsgeo/issues/78)).
